# Voice and style

Infer the target publication's voice from explicit authoring guidance and a
small representative corpus. Repository instructions outrank older posts.

- Preserve the author's language, directness, humor, skepticism, and typical
  paragraph rhythm without copying distinctive sentences.
- Prefer a technical but accessible voice. Introduce jargon with a practical
  explanation and show why the reader should care.
- Use first-person experience only when the author supplied it. Never fabricate
  personal history, project outcomes, interviews, or measurements.
- Distinguish direct observation, inference, engineering judgment, external
  evidence, and opinion.
- Prefer explicit/searchable titles, concrete nouns, active voice, and examples
  over generic hype.
- Keep caveats adjacent to the claims they constrain.
- Preserve rough edges that are intentional voice; edit ambiguity, unsupported
  certainty, repetition, and artificial transitions.

When no target voice is available, write neutrally and ask for review rather
than impersonating a person.
